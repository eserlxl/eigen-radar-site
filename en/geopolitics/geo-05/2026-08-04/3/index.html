What a decree can reach from Geneva

17:35 UTC
1.2×

Two lists

The decrees signed on Monday from Geneva appointed officials to key army positions and raised five colonels to brigadier general. One of them, Raymond Jean Charles Beko'o Abondo, was immediately reappointed commander of the presidential guard, the unit responsible for protecting the president, which he has led since 2013. The list confirms people who are already where they are.[1]

The second list is what the decrees left alone. A cabinet promised after last year's presidential election has not been named. A vice-presidency created in April is still unfilled. Both would require choosing among people who would then hold power inside the country, which is a heavier act than confirming an officer already in command.[1]

A quieter reading fits the same facts. Both appointments may simply be files a president prefers to sign at home, or ones held back for a political moment that has not arrived. What can be said from the reporting is narrower: during nearly 60 days abroad the decree channel has carried army promotions and authorisations for his government to sign loan agreements with international financial institutions, and it has not carried the two appointments that would redistribute domestic authority.[1]

Who is contesting it?

Communication Minister Rene Emmanuel Sadi told the French-language broadcaster RFI that the president is alive, will return soon and continues to work from Geneva, and rejected the opposition's claim that the absence amounts to a vacancy at the head of state. Mamadou Mota, vice-president of the opposition CRM, said last month that the country faced a glaring institutional vacuum, and that executive power is exercised at the heart of the nation rather than through smoke signals or telepathy from hotel suites on the other side of the world.[1]

The argument is being conducted with one instrument removed from the public side of it. Reporting on the president's health has been banned in Cameroon since an announcement two years ago, so the question that would settle the dispute between Sadi and Mota is the one Cameroonian journalists cannot put in print. That constraint shapes what each side is able to assert and what neither can be held to.[1]

A habit, or a signal?

Three earlier reshuffles are reported: after the 2023 coup in neighbouring Gabon, during last year's campaign for an eighth term, and again in June. Read against those three, Monday's decrees are the fourth instance of a familiar move by a government that reaches for the army list when it is being watched closely. On its own it is thin evidence of anything new.[1]

What separates habit from signal is the other list, and Cameroonians can watch it without any access to Geneva. If the cabinet is named or the vice-presidency filled while Biya is still abroad, the decree channel is doing more than confirming the army's top ranks. If neither happens before Biya returns, the loan authorisations signed abroad are the more consequential paper of these two months, and the promotions are the visible surface of a continuity being managed rather than announced.[1]
