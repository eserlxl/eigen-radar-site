Bamako's answer to San was a video

15:28 UTC
1.2×

Two accounts of one camp

Mali's army says it repelled Sunday's assault on the regiment's camp at San and killed around 40 fighters. The garrison commander, Lt Col El Oumrani, made the claim in a video, urging residents to stay vigilant and insisting the camp remained in army custody. The same account concedes that his soldiers had to retreat temporarily.[1]

JNIM, the al-Qaeda-affiliated group behind the attack, says its fighters took control of the barracks. Witnesses quoted by the French broadcaster RFI describe an assault that began around 05:00 local time and lasted about two hours, with attackers arriving on motorcycles and on foot, some in army uniforms. One said the militants held the camp for a long time before leaving. AFP, citing security and local sources, reported at least 10 soldiers killed and a brief takeover.[1]

The army also told the national broadcaster ORTM that the attackers breached the site's perimeter with drones, heavy machine guns and armoured vehicles. A force arriving with armour and drones describes a sustained assault, and that sits awkwardly beside a message meant to calm the town.[1]

What the 2020 choice promised

Mali's rejection of French military help after the coup was a choice among credible alternatives, and Bamako made it in public: French forces out, Russia in their place. The coup itself was carried out in part over the failure to ease the security situation, so the new orientation arrived with an explicit measure of success. It was meant to deliver what the foreign deployment had not.[1]

Measured against that promise, the map has moved the wrong way. Large parts of the north and east have stayed outside government control since 2012. In April an alliance of jihadists and the separatist Azawad Liberation Front in the north mounted co-ordinated attacks across the country aimed at toppling Gen Assimi Goita's military regime, and Defence Minister Sadio Camara was killed in an apparent suicide truck bombing at his residence near Bamako. JNIM has since diversified its operations to target central and southern Mali as well.[1]

That southward movement is where San sits, and where the claim of control is tested. A government that took power on a security argument answered a two-hour assault with its commander's video, and no figure for its own dead followed. A simpler reading should stay in view: a garrison commander addressing residents he is urging to stay vigilant has reasons to project calm that have nothing to do with the balance of control, and the army did report casualties, only without a number.[1]

What would settle it?

One observable signal is cheap to check. If Mali's authorities publish their own count of soldiers killed at San by the end of September 2026, the state will have contested the account of the battle with numbers of its own. If the only figure available by then is still the one AFP attributes to security and local sources, the government's claim of control will rest largely on narration.[1]

The wider question is who in Mali gets to speak for the state's presence. At San it was a regimental commander on video; the defence ministry that would normally answer lost its minister in April. Residents heard the reassurance from the garrison, while the fighting they described to RFI had taken place 200 to 300 metres from the camp.[1]
