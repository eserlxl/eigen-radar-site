Gravity readings put Comox Glacier's ice at 42 m along one survey line

A Canadian team used gravity readings to estimate the ice thickness along a line across Comox Glacier on Vancouver Island. The ice averaged 42 m on a line 450 m long. At British Columbia’s Kokanee Glacier the same approach broadly agreed with an earlier radar survey. The Comox measurements give future surveys a baseline for that part of the glacier.

X-rays reveal which way mineral grains lie inside rock

A team at the University of Sydney used X-ray scans to measure how spinel grains line up inside a rock without cutting it open. Its three-dimensional method screens out grains too small for the scanner to resolve reliably. The alignment of mineral grains can hold clues to how rock was deformed or how molten rock once flowed.

Mature lychee shoots flowered earlier after winter cooling

On lychee trees in China's Jiangxi province, around 91 per cent of shoots that started winter mature went on to flower. About 30 per cent of the shoots still growing at the start did so. Mature shoots also formed buds earlier, showing how much the flowering response to winter cooling can vary within a single tree.

A team in China tested a smaller instrument that reads oxygen's light from about 250 kilometres above Earth to measure wind and temperature from the ground.
