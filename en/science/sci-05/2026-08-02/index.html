Ultrathin RuO₂ has a spin texture, but its order is not yet named

Updated 17:23 UTC
1.2×

What was measured and what was inferred

The measurement was made with spin-resolved angle-resolved photoemission spectroscopy on RuO₂ heterostructures 2.7 nanometres thick, grown under strain imposed by the substrate. What was observed is a momentum-dependent spin texture: mirror-odd and mirror-even components coexisting. The inference sits one step beyond that: a comprehensive symmetry analysis says the texture cannot come from non-magnetic origins. The magnetic character is reached by exclusion rather than measured directly.[1]

A conclusion reached by exclusion is only as strong as the list of options excluded. Here the list is public: the symmetry analysis was published and is supported by calculations from first principles. What remains undetermined is which magnetic order the texture belongs to, that is, weak ferromagnetism or altermagnetism. The measurement closes one door without separating the two rooms behind it.[1]

Where the result's limits sit

Two limits set the scope of the result. The first is thickness: accumulated experimental evidence points to RuO₂ showing no magnetic ordering in bulk or thick-film form, and this work does not change that picture. The new measurement therefore does not restore the earlier claims about bulk material; it points to a separate state that strain brings out in the ultrathin limit. The second is temperature: the measurements were made at about 15 kelvin, and whether the same texture persists at room temperature is unknown.[1]

Read together, those two limits produce a narrowing in place of a refutation. Earlier rounds of the dispute asked whether RuO₂ is an altermagnet; the question this result licenses becomes what ultrathin, strained RuO₂ shows. As a claim's scope shrinks its testability grows, and that does not count as a loss.[1]

What would end the dispute

The measurement that would draw the distinction is one that carries temperature upward on films of the same thickness: how far the texture persists above 15 kelvin would supply what today's data cannot say about which order is at work. The second requirement is an independent group repeating the spin-resolved measurement under the same strain and thickness conditions. Until both are done, the accurate sentence is this: a spin texture of magnetic origin was measured in thin, strained RuO₂, and its order has not yet been named.[1]
